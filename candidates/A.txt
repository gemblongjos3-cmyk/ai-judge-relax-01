The kettle has finished, and the kitchen ticks as it cools. Outside the window the street is empty except for one porch light, amber and patient. A cat has folded itself into the warm square the radiator leaves on the rug. Nothing here needs an answer. The clock moves the way a river moves when nobody is watching it — one second, then another, in no hurry at all. You can leave the day where it fell, on the table beside the keys. It will keep. The light will keep. And in the quiet that is left, there is nowhere else to be.
